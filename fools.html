Listen, idiots

An arrogant creator (laugh)

Development has become more accessible, but capitalism lurks behind it.

Because of this goddamn capitalism, most of what’s offered as a development environment comes with strings attached. They’ll let you use it for free, sure — but they’re telling you to endure a certain amount, to compromise. For example, they can force you to slap a corporate logo onto the finished work you built with it.

Yeah, compromise matters. But when it comes to your own work, you want to obsess over it down to the last detail, don’t you? If that’s the case, there’s a limit to how much you can ride on things born out of capitalism. In the end, the one who creates from absolute zero is the smartest, the most admirable.

Damn it, it’s obvious — and yet you still find yourself wanting to lean on what’s already there. Either way, learning the skills takes too much time and too much money.

Behind the words “I want to do it” there’s also “I want to, but I can’t.”

Does that sound like an excuse?

There are reasons people can’t. If all you want is to stand above someone, then sure, go ahead and hurl that lie at them.

If you really want to stir someone into action, what we — outsiders — can do is either give them the assets and the time they need to start moving, or leave them alone. If you’re going to help, support them to the extent you actually can. If you can’t help, ignore them.

That’s what kindness is. It’s not something that can be fixed with lectures or pretty words, you self-important game creators.

And then, before you can even shape your idea into form, someone beats you to it.

You have to work efficiently, cleanly — everything, absolutely everything.

In the end, a work is born from a cycle of compromise and efficiency. That’s why creating and releasing a single piece is an incredible thing.

If you made it to release, be proud.

If you’re still rowing toward it, don’t give up — but don’t overdo it either.

If you haven’t even boarded the ship yet, either keep agonizing over it or throw it away.

Looking down on others only ever gives you a warped sense of superiority. It produces nothing.
